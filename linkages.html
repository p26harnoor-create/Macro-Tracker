§4.1

The master chain

#

One diagram for the whole regime. Two streams — the US/dollar stream and the Indian supply stream — meet at Indian CPI, and the RBI is where they are resolved.

Stream A · Dollar / rates
US fiscal concerns + oil inflation→US 10Y/30Y ↑ (term premium)→Treasuries attractive→USD ↑→INR ↓→rupee cost of oil, edible oils, fertilizer, electronics ↑→WPI / input costs ↑
Stream B · Indian supply
Hormuz → Brent, LNG, urea ↑+onion, garlic, ginger, sugar, edible oils→food CPI 5.95%+fuel & power WPI +23%
Where they meet
Headline CPI 4.82%, food 5.95%butWPI 9.92%→Will upstream pressure fade before it passes into retail, wages and services?→RBI: hike into a supply shock to prevent Stage 3→India yields ↑, growth/equity valuation pressure

View The variable at the very start is the US long end, not DXY. If US long yields sustainably retreat while oil keeps falling, most of India's external pressure unwinds before the RBI does anything dramatic.

§4.3

The feedback loops the RBI is trying to keep orderly

#

Oil ↔ INR

Oil ↑ → import bill ↑ → USD demand ↑ → INR ↓ → oil dearer in rupees → imported inflation ↑ → RBI hawkish → conditions tighten. Oil hurts INR, and INR amplifies oil.

FPI ↔ INR

Equity −5% and INR −5% = −10% in USD → more selling → more INR sold → INR ↓ → worse USD returns. Equity selling is FX selling.

Yields ↔ deficit (US)

Deficit → supply → yields ↑ → interest bill ↑ → deficit ↑. The structural engine under the 5% long end.

Intervention ↔ liquidity

RBI sells USD to smooth INR → drains rupees → tightens → while FCNR(B) adds rupees and OMO sales drain them. Three dials, one hand.

The dangerous scenario (view)US 10Y > 5.5% · Brent > $110 · DXY materially higher · FPI selling continues → USD/INR ↑ → intervention ↑ → imported inflation ↑ → RBI hawkishness ↑ → India yields ↑ → equities ↓ → FPI selling ↑ → INR ↓ again.
The positive loop (view)US 10Y 5.3% → 4.5% · Brent $100 → $85–90 · FPI selling stops · INR stabilises → flows return → inflation pressure ↓ → RBI less hawkish → yields ↓ → P/E expands.
§4.4

Cyclical vs structural

#

The single most useful analytical habit from the chat: separate what is a 2026 shock from what is a multi-year shift. Don't mix the time horizons.

TopicCyclical (2026–27)Structural (2027–31+)
FertilizerHormuz → urea prices → subsidy shockNIPU-2026 → domestic plants → lower import dependence; green ammonia optionality
Indian inflationOil, INR, onions, edible oils, sugarProtein-demand shift with rising incomes; fertilizer self-reliance lowers volatility
INRUS yields, oil, FPI outflows, euro-led DXYServices exports, remittances, import substitution (fertilizer, electronics, defence, semis) lower structural USD demand
Indian equitiesFPI outflows, oil, US yields, RBI hikesDomestic SIPs, private capex, infrastructure, manufacturing
Indian bondsHike pricing, OMO sales, H2 supply, thin foreign bidFiscal consolidation path; index inclusion; deepening domestic institutional demand
US TreasuriesFed, NFP, CPI, oilDeficit, issuance, term premium, buyer-base shift
OilHormuz closures/reopeningsChokepoint risk premium; India's energy-mix and strategic-reserve policy
