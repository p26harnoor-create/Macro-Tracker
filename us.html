Chapter 03 · United States

United States: a hiking Fed, a 5% long end, and a $2 trillion deficit

The US matters to India through one channel above all: the level of long Treasury yields. This chapter explains why the 10Y is at a 19-year high even as Fed-hike expectations ease, and what would bring it down. Added sections are built from current reporting; the dollar/yield mechanics come from the shared chat.

Research as of 6 Oct 2026. Next FOMC: 27–28 Oct.

§3.1

The Fed: first hike since 2023 Added

#

On 16 September the FOMC under Chair Kevin Warsh raised the target range 25 bp to 3.75–4.00%, unanimously, and the dots signalled one more hike in 2026 — a response to oil-driven inflation that had been "too high for too long."

3.75–4.00%Fed funds after +25 bp, 16 Sept; 12–0 vote
4.1%Dot-plot median for end-2026 and 2027
3.4%CPI YoY Aug; +0.4% m/m, biggest in four months
4.1%Unemployment Aug; payrolls +162k
27–28 OctNext FOMC

Fact Warsh (chair since May 2026): "The plain fact is that inflation is too high, and has been for too long." The Fed had been on hold from late 2025; this was the first increase since July 2023. Drivers cited: energy (crude >$100, diesel >$6/gal) and a labour market resilient enough to tolerate tightening.

View What matters for India is the asymmetry: markets have since pared expectations of further hikes (the September payrolls were weak), yet long yields stayed near highs. The Fed is no longer the marginal driver of the 10Y — the fiscal/term-premium story is (§3.2). That is why a Fed pause alone would not rescue EM assets.

Why hike into an oil shock?

Classic doctrine says look through supply shocks. The FOMC chose not to because (a) inflation had been above target for five years, so expectations were fragile; (b) the labour market was not weak enough to do the work; (c) the Hormuz shock was persisting, not fading — Brent re-crossed $100 in July after the June MoU collapsed. The hike is a credibility purchase, the same logic the RBI is weighing (India §2.1).

What the market has done since

  • Early-October payrolls: private +46k, government −17k — soft enough to cut odds of a December hike.
  • Yet 10Y 5.28% (week to 3 Oct, +11 bp) and 30Y 5.63% (5.69% intraday, highest since 2002). Front-end hike pricing eased; the long end did not follow.
  • 6 Oct: oil −3%, 10Y back to ~5.27% — the first good day for duration in a while.

The policy mix problem

View A hawkish Fed plus $2 trillion deficits is the worst mix for EM: it supports the dollar through both the policy rate and the term premium. The best outcome for India is not a dovish Fed; it is a Fed that is done and a Treasury market that believes supply is under control.

Oil shock → CPI 3.4%→Fed hikes 25 bp→front end ↑→USD supported→EM hurdle rate ↑
Weak payrolls→hike odds ↓→2Y eases→but 10Y/30Y stay high (term premium)→limited EM relief
  • mid-OctSeptember CPI (BLS) and PCE later in the month.
  • 27–28 OctFOMC: hold vs second hike; any change to the "one more" dot.
  • early NovTreasury quarterly refunding — coupon sizes are the term-premium event.

For India: good Fed outcome

  • Hold with "data-dependent" language; oil easing cited

Bad Fed outcome

§3.2

The long end: 10Y 5.3%, 30Y 5.6%, highest since 2002–07

#

Long yields are high for fiscal and supply reasons as much as for the Fed. This is the regime shift at the top of every India mechanism chain.

5.28%10Y close, 3 Oct; 5.36% intraday high; ~5.27% on 6 Oct
5.63%30Y close; 5.69% intraday — highest since 2002
~$41 trnFederal debt
>$2 trnAnnual deficit

Fact The 10Y crossed 5% on 14 Sept ahead of the FOMC and hit a 19-year high in late September. It eased on 6 Oct as oil fell ~3%, trimming inflation fears, but remains near multi-decade highs.

View The market is no longer saying "the Fed will hike forever." It is saying "US long-duration assets need a higher risk premium for deficits, inflation uncertainty and Treasury supply." That keeps yields high → dollar supported → global tightening, even if the Fed itself turns less hawkish. For India, the 10Y/30Y is the variable at the very start of the chain — more than DXY.

Why supply matters more now

The Fed is not buying (QT); foreign official demand is flat; so the marginal buyer is private and price-sensitive. Each refunding tests how much coupon issuance the market will absorb without a concession. The 30Y at 5.69% intraday is that concession being demanded.

Old vs new framework

Old: Fed stops hiking → yields ↓ → USD ↓ → EM rally. Now: Fed stops hiking, but supply and term premium keep the 10Y ~5% → USD attractive → EM relief limited. Treasury buybacks and issuance skew toward bills can ease the long end at the margin; they do not change the arithmetic of a $2 trillion deficit.

Deficit > $2 trn + QT→coupon supply ↑→price-sensitive buyers demand premium→term premium ↑→10Y/30Y ↑ regardless of Fed→USD ↑→INR ↓, FPI exits, India yields ↑

  • Quarterly refunding (early Nov): coupon sizes, bill share, buyback schedule.
  • SF Fed / NY Fed term-premium estimates.
  • Oil: the 6 Oct move showed the long end still trades inflation, not only supply.

§3.3

Fiscal: the structural layer under the yields Added

#

Debt near $41 trillion and deficits above $2 trillion a year mean Treasury supply is the structural force behind the long end — the cyclical Fed story sits on top of it.

Fact Federal debt is approaching $41 trillion with annual deficits above $2 trillion; the US also gained roughly $50bn of extra export revenue during the war period from higher energy prices — a reminder that the US is a net energy exporter and India a net importer. The same oil shock improves the US external account and worsens India's.

View Cyclical vs structural for Treasuries: cyclical = Fed, NFP, CPI; structural = deficit, issuance, term premium. Interest costs rising with yields create their own feedback: higher yields → bigger interest bill → bigger deficit → more supply.

Deficit > $2 trn→issuance ↑→yields ↑→interest bill ↑→deficit ↑↺
Oil ↑→US export revenue ↑ (net exporter)vsIndia import bill ↑ (net importer)→USD/INR ↑
  • CBO monthly budget review; interest outlays as % of revenue.
  • Refunding statements; bill vs coupon mix.
  • Any fiscal package / tariff-revenue developments.

CPI 3.4% YoY in August with a 0.4% monthly jump, diesel above $6, and OECD/Fed forecasts marked up on Hormuz. Unlike India, the US shock is energy-led rather than food-led — but the second-round risk is the same question.

§3.5

Labour: slowing, not breaking Added

#

Unemployment 4.1% and +162k payrolls in August were resilient enough to permit a hike; the early-October report (private +46k, government −17k) was soft enough to make a second hike less certain.

Fact August: unemployment 4.1% (unchanged), payrolls +162k. Early-October release: private +46k, government −17k.

View For India, a soft US labour market is double-edged: it lowers Fed-hike odds (good for INR via the front end) but, if it tips into recession fears, it strengthens the dollar as a haven and hits risk appetite (bad for FPI flows). The benign path is "cooling, not cracking."

  • Monthly NFP, unemployment rate, average hourly earnings (wage inflation = second-round gauge).
  • JOLTS quits, initial claims.

§3.6

The dollar: DXY is a euro index

#

DXY measures the dollar against the euro (by far the largest weight), yen, sterling, CAD, SEK and CHF. It can rise because Europe weakens — and still move USD/INR.

Fact Reuters described early-October rupee pressure as partly "euro-led dollar strength." DXY contains no EM currencies.

View Separate two dollars: the DM dollar (DXY, driven by ECB/BoJ vs Fed) and the EM dollar (driven by US yields, risk appetite, oil, China). India's problem is the second, with the first adding noise. Watch the broad trade-weighted dollar and the EM FX index alongside DXY.

EUR sells off→DXY ↑→Asian FX dealers sell local currencies→USD/INR ↑ "for free"
US yields ↑ + oil ↑ + risk-off→EM dollar ↑→USD/INR ↑ "for real"
Sources: Reuters via Business Recorder (6 Oct)ICE DXY methodology · ChatGPT deep dive.
§3.7

Equities & AI capex: a 5% risk-free rate tests the multiple Added

#

With Treasuries at 5.3%, the equity risk premium is thin. The AI-capex cycle is the structural support; rates and oil are the cyclical test.

View The same hurdle-rate logic that hits Indian equities hits US equities: when the risk-free rate is 5.3%, a 20×+ forward multiple needs earnings growth to justify it. AI capex (hyperscaler data-centre spend, chips, power) is where that growth is concentrated, which makes the index unusually dependent on a handful of names and on the power/semiconductor supply chain — including Indian IT services and Indian data-centre build-out as second-order beneficiaries.

§3.8

Gold: resisting a hostile macro

#

Strong dollar plus high real yields should be bad for gold. It remains very elevated — which says geopolitics, fiscal worry and central-bank buying are overriding the textbook.

View Gold pays no coupon, so high real yields raise its opportunity cost. That it has not fallen is a signal of hedge demand against sovereign/fiscal risk and war. For India, gold is also a CPI item (gold jewellery +35.5%, silver +107% YoY in August) and an import — rupee weakness lifts domestic prices even when USD gold is flat, and higher gold imports add to dollar demand.

Real yields ↑, USD ↑→textbook: gold ↓vswar + fiscal risk + central-bank buying→gold stays high
USD gold × USD/INR + duty→Indian gold price→CPI jewellery ↑, import bill ↑
