§6.1

30-second answers

#
What's driving Indian inflation right now?

Five interacting shocks, mostly supply-side: food dispersion (onion +48%, garlic +44%, ginger +74% while tomato −31%), oil (WPI fuel & power +23%), a weak rupee near 96, industrial input costs (WPI manufactured +8.4%), and the risk of second-round effects. Headline CPI is 4.82% but WPI is 9.92% — that gap is the story. India is between "shock" and "producer inflation"; the question is whether it reaches wages and services. Housing at 2.3% says this is not broad overheating. §2.2

What has the RBI done in October, and what next?

Three things. 7 Oct: hiked 25 bp to 5.50% (unanimous), stance to "calibrated tightening" (4–2), FY27 CPI 5.2% (Q3 6.0%), GDP 7.1%; "hike or pause," cuts off the table. 10 Oct: an FX/liquidity package — special dollar window for IOC/HPCL/BPCL, 20% FX risk reserve on INR hedges, no-underlying hedge cap cut to $5 mn, ₹25,000 cr OMO sale, daily CRR floor to 99% — which took the rupee from 96.7 to 94.3. 21 Oct: minutes showed Kumar and Singh dissenting on the stance (supply-driven inflation; risk to investment) while DG Gupta said a further hike "may be needed." With September CPI at 5.54% and WPI 11.39%, December (2–4 Dec) is a hike unless oil breaks. §2.1

What did the RBI do on 7 October specifically?

Hiked 25 bp to 5.50% (unanimous) — the first hike in nearly four years — and moved the stance from neutral to "calibrated tightening" (4–2 on the stance). FY27 CPI raised to 5.2% with Q3 at 6.0%; FY27 GDP raised to 7.1% (Q1 printed 7.8%). Core ex-precious-metals at 2.9% is the "not yet generalised" evidence. Guidance: "the policy action ahead can only be a rate hike or a pause"; cuts off the table. Market: 10Y +5 bp to 7.27%, rupee flat at 96.36, Nifty −0.6%. The next day oil and US yields did the damage, not the RBI. Goldman: hikes in December and February. §2.1

Why would the RBI hike if it can't produce oil or onions?

Second-round effects and expectations. If households assume 6–7% inflation, wage demands and pre-emptive price rises make it self-fulfilling. The government fights the source (duty cuts, stock releases); the RBI stops the spread. A hike also narrows the rate gap with 5.3% Treasuries, which helps the rupee. The RBI had already tightened via liquidity (VRRR, OMO sales, FX sales) before the 7 Oct repo hike. §2.1

What's happening with the rupee?

It broke 97 on 8 October (97.13), the day after the RBI hiked — because four forces fired together: 5.35% US Treasuries raising the hurdle rate for all EM assets; euro-led dollar strength (DXY is a euro index); $105 oil creating refiner dollar demand; and FPI selling (₹31,282 cr in eight sessions), which is itself FX selling. A 25 bp hike cannot offset a simultaneous move in oil and the US long end; the RBI smooths the path, not the level. The variable at the top of the chain is the US long end, not DXY. §2.4

What's happening with fertilizer?

Two stories. Cyclical: Hormuz raised gas, urea and shipping costs while India imports ~25% of its urea; because farm prices are subsidised, that showed up as fiscal pressure, not CPI. Structural: NIPU-2026 (approved 15 July) tries to end that exposure with a 12–16% ROE band, fixed/variable cost separation and an FX fix; ~₹2.04 trillion of proposals for 8–9 plants. The test is whether proposals become commissioned capacity. §2.3

Why are US long yields at 19-year highs if Fed-hike odds are falling?

Because the long end is pricing fiscal risk and supply, not just the Fed: debt ~$41 trillion, deficits >$2 trillion, QT, a price-sensitive marginal buyer. The term premium is the swing factor. That is why a Fed pause alone may not rescue EM: the 10Y can stay ~5% and the dollar stays supported. §3.2

Why did the Fed hike into an oil shock?

Inflation had been above target for five years, so expectations were fragile; the labour market (4.1% unemployment, +162k) could take it; and the Hormuz shock persisted. Warsh: inflation "too high, and has been for too long." 25 bp to 3.75–4.00% on 16 Sept, 12–0, dots at 4.1% for 2026–27. §3.1

What did FCNR(B) do?

Raised $127bn (4.9× 2013) of 3–5 year NRI dollar deposits with RBI-subsidised hedging and CRR/SLR exemptions. It gave record reserves ($740.8bn) and rupee support — and a record ₹11.6 lakh crore liquidity surplus that the RBI is draining with VRRR and OMO sales. That drain is why short rates and the 10Y rose before any repo move. §2.5

Why is the India 10Y at 7.21% with the repo unchanged?

Three forces on the long end: hike pricing (1Y OIS 6.21%), duration supply (~₹8 trillion H2 issuance plus RBI OMO sales), and a thinner foreign bid as the hedged spread to 5.3% Treasuries compresses. It is a pressure range (7.4–7.5% if it persists), not a fiscal-credibility event. §2.6

What happened on 8 October?

An oil-and-rates sell-off, not an earnings one: Brent +5% to $105.30 on US–Iran escalation reports and Houthi strikes on Saudi airports; US 10Y 5.35% after FOMC minutes and Waller pointed to more hikes; USD/INR 97.13; FPIs still selling; the RBI hike repricing rate-sensitives. Sensex −1,045 (−1.44%) to 71,593, Nifty −371 (−1.64%) to 22,231, India VIX +10%, Metal and Realty −3%+, mid/small caps −2%+. Gainers: Infosys, HCL Tech, Axis Bank — the IT/USD-revenue hedge worked. §2.7

Why hasn't the Nifty fallen more given FPI selling?

Domestic institutions. FPIs measure returns in dollars and are hit twice (P/E compression from 5.3% Treasuries, plus INR). DIIs measure in rupees; SIP inflows are sticky. FPI sells / DII buys is the defining pattern. §2.7

Where does the India–US trade deal stand?

February 2026 framework cut US tariffs on Indian goods from 50% to 18% and removed the Russian-oil penalty. Both sides confirm the 18%; Delhi has not confirmed zero tariffs, the $500bn purchase target or ending Russian oil. The legal text is still pending; India says it hinges on a preferential rate. §2.9

What's the state of India's power sector, and why does a macro desk care?

Demand is running +11% with a record 270.8 GW peak; hydro fell 20% on the monsoon and LNG at $26 idled the gas fleet, so coal PLF hit 69% and plant stocks fell to 36% of norm — evening-peak shortfalls topped 7 GW in September and IEX prices doubled (₹7.3/unit). Discoms owe gencos ₹85,700 cr again. The build-out is the biggest capex pipeline in the economy — ₹9.16 lakh cr of transmission to 2032, 44 GW of coal under construction, 290 GWh of storage tendered, nuclear to 100 GW by 2047 — but transmission is 42% behind its ckm target and 44.8 GW of RE has no PPA. Macro links: coal imports add to the CAD/INR chain, power costs feed WPI and state budgets, and every quarter of delay at a 7.46% 10Y is a cost-of-capital event. §2.11

Telecom in one minute?

Three-player market: Jio 39%, Airtel 38%, Vi 15% of 1.31 bn subscribers, both leaders adding ~2 mn a month; ARPU ₹264/₹216/₹195; 5G fixed-wireless 13.5 mn homes. No tariff hike since July 2024 despite expectations of 16–20% in 2026. Jio Platforms' ₹30,213 cr IPO (largest ever, ~₹10.3 lakh cr mcap) is 4.4× subscribed — ~₹3 trn of application money locked until ~27 Oct is a liquidity event in an already-drained system. Vi's AGR dues were cut 27% to ₹64,046 cr and an SBI-led $3.5bn package unlocks ₹45,000 cr of capex (equipment-import heavy, INR-sensitive). Satcom pricing is at Cabinet; no spectrum auction in 2026. §2.12

What are the headwinds for India right now?

Ten, ranked: (1) oil at $110–112 with Hormuz closed — CAD, rupee, subsidies, WPI 11.4%; (2) record FPI outflows ₹3.12 lakh cr and reserves down $60bn; (3) inflation 5.54% → calibrated tightening, 10Y 7.46%; (4) fiscal slippage — deficit 41.9% of BE by August, subsidies +24%, fertilizer/LPG overshoot ₹1.2–1.4 lakh cr; (5) monsoon 13% deficient with El Niño peaking; (6) private capex announcements −49% QoQ; (7) the unfinished US deal and the reversible Russia-oil tariff waiver; (8) bank NIM compression (HDFC 3.16%) with credit outpacing deposits; (9) Chinese steel dumping; (10) the evening power deficit. Against them: Q1 GDP 7.8% with GFCF 34.3% of GDP, public capex +18%, GST +14.7%, remittances $43bn a quarter, DII buying ₹6.8 trn — which is why IMF, World Bank and RBI all upgraded to 7.0–7.1% in October. The gap between upgraded forecasts and deteriorating flow data is the story. §2.13

Is India's private capex cycle real?

Real but pausing. RBI's September Bulletin: ₹3.2 trn planned for FY27 (from ₹2.6 trn), record ₹4.4 trn sanctioned in FY26, distress under 0.1%, industrial bank credit +₹6 lakh cr; Q1 FY27 GFCF +11.9% real to 34.3% of GDP. But CMIE's September quarter shows private announcements −49% QoQ (manufacturing −32% YoY), capacity utilisation slipping to 75.2% SA, and capital-goods orders ex-L&T −10%. Public capex (+18%) and data centres (now 4.5% of GDP of investment, Nomura) are carrying the cycle while private manufacturing waits out oil, rates (10Y 7.46%) and input-cost inflation. §2.8

How big is US AI capex, and why does it matter?

$720–745bn of 2026 capex guidance across Alphabet ($195–205bn), Amazon (~$220bn), Microsoft (~$175bn) and Meta ($130–145bn); ~$303bn spent in H1; all guiding higher for 2027. It is why the S&P sat at record highs with a 5.3% 10Y — earnings growth is concentrated in the build-out. Risks: cash leaves before costs hit earnings (Amazon FCF −$7.6bn), useful-life accounting, power constraints, concentration. India angle: IT services, GCCs, domestic data-centre capex. §3.7

Why is gold high if real yields and the dollar are high?

It's less high than it was: ~$4,120 on 8 Oct, a nine-week low the day before, and about 20% below its pre-war level. Through the summer it resisted the textbook on hedge demand against war and fiscal risk plus central-bank buying; the retracement says real yields are now winning. For India, gold × USD/INR means domestic prices rise even when USD gold is flat — showing up in CPI (jewellery +35.5%, silver +107%) and the import bill. §3.8

§6.3

Questions to expect

#
  • Is Indian inflation a demand or a supply problem — and what follows for the RBI?
  • Walk me from the US 10Y to the Nifty.
  • If the Fed pauses, does the rupee recover? (Not necessarily — term premium.)
  • What does FX intervention do to liquidity? What offsets it?
  • What's the difference between DXY and the dollar India faces?
  • Why is WPI at 10% and CPI at 5%? Which closes toward which?
  • What would make you bullish on INR / Indian duration / Nifty?
  • Fertilizer: cyclical or structural? What's the KPI?
  • Is the SIP bid a valuation argument? (No — a cushion.)
  • What's the one number you'd put on a dashboard? (Brent × USD/INR.)
§6.4

Glossary

#
CPI / WPIConsumer vs wholesale price index. The gap between them measures upstream cost pressure not yet passed to consumers.
CFPIConsumer Food Price Index — the food component of CPI.
Second-round effectsWhen a supply shock (oil, food) feeds into wages and services prices, generalising inflation.
Pass-throughThe share of a cost or exchange-rate shock that reaches final prices, and how fast.
Term premiumExtra yield investors demand for holding long bonds beyond expected short rates — driven by supply, inflation uncertainty, fiscal risk.
DXYICE Dollar Index: USD vs EUR (largest weight), JPY, GBP, CAD, SEK, CHF. Contains no EM currencies.
FCNR(B)Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits — NRI deposits held in foreign currency; the 2026 special window raised $127bn.
VRRRVariable Rate Reverse Repo — RBI auction to absorb surplus liquidity for a term.
OMO salesRBI selling government bonds outright to drain liquidity — also adds duration supply.
OISOvernight Index Swap — the cleanest read of how many policy moves the market prices.
Crowding outGovernment borrowing absorbing savings and raising yields, squeezing private investment.
FPI / DIIForeign portfolio investors vs domestic institutional investors (mutual funds, insurers, pensions).
SIPSystematic Investment Plan — monthly retail flows into mutual funds; India's structural equity bid.
NIPU-2026National Investment Policy for Urea 2026 — CCEA-approved 15 Jul 2026; 12–16% ROE band, fixed/variable cost separation, FX conversion after four years.
MSP / FCIMinimum Support Price; Food Corporation of India — the state's grain procurement and stock machinery.
Rupee oil priceBrent × USD/INR — India's effective imported oil cost.
Bear-flattenerYields rise, short end more than long — the pattern when hikes get priced.
